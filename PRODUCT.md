# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences share one system, in Thai. On the firm side, staff at an audit / accounting practice run engagements: they generate a document request list (PBC — prepared-by-client) from a template, watch uploads arrive, review and accept or return each item, comment, and track where every request stands. They are repeat power users who live in the dashboard and want density, status at a glance, and fast review.

On the client side are the audited company's staff, who arrive through a login-less link plus a code. Their job is narrow and occasional: see what the firm asked for, upload the right files, and know they did it correctly. They are not trained on the tool and should never need to be — their side must feel simple, low-pressure, and obviously safe.

## Product Purpose

Tickmark is the single place an audit firm and its client run the back-and-forth of collecting engagement documents: request → client upload → firm review → accepted, with status, comments, reminders, and an audit-friendly trail throughout. It exists to end the scattered email-and-spreadsheet chase for PBC documents. Success is a firm that can see, without asking, exactly which documents are in, which are late, and which need attention — and a client who can respond without confusion or a phone call.

## Positioning

One portal that turns "please send us these documents" into a tracked, two-way workflow — request, upload, review, accept — instead of an email thread nobody can reconstruct.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, professional, precise. Calm competence over marketing flash. For the client, the interface should read as secure and official — the feeling of handing documents to a serious firm through a proper channel, not a casual web app. Confidence comes from exactness and legibility (accurate statuses, dates, counts, clear labels), never from decoration.

## Anti-references

Not generic template SaaS — no rainbow gradients, no endless identical icon-heading-text card grids, no big hero-metric vanity blocks. Not a stiff, outdated government or legacy-ERP system — dense, cramped, gray, hostile. And not playful or toy-like — no candy colors, cartoon illustration, or emoji-as-personality that would undercut trust in a financial context. The existing navy/mint system is the reference to stay true to.

## Design Principles

Trust through precision: every status chip, date, count, and progress figure is exact and highly legible, because in audit the number is the point. Ambiguity reads as unreliability.

Calm over flash: the design serves the workflow and gets out of the way. Reach for clarity, whitespace, and restraint before any decorative device; if an effect doesn't help someone find or finish a task, it doesn't ship.

Two audiences, one system: honor the firm's need for density and speed and the client's need for simplicity and reassurance without splintering into two disconnected looks. Same visual language, different information load.

Serious, never stuffy: professional and credible without tipping into the cramped, dated government-system feel. Modern restraint is the line to walk.

Thai-first, bilingual-ready: Thai is the primary language; typography, spacing, and line-height must handle Thai and Latin together cleanly, and copy stays plain and human.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard readable accessibility, not a formal certification target. Body text meets ≥4.5:1 contrast (no light-gray-on-tint), interactive controls are keyboard-reachable with visible focus, and touch targets are adequate on mobile. No known assistive-tech mandate, but nothing should present an obvious barrier.
